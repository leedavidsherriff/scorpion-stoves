Fitting a stove or fireplace

Do I need a working chimney?

No. With no chimney, Gareth runs a black twin-wall flue out through the wall and up the side of the house (£3,600–£3,800, cylinder stove included). In a single-storey extension the flue goes straight up through the roof (£2,300–£2,500). If your chimney stack has already been taken down, that’s a straight stove fit too (£1,375, stove extra). The free survey tells you which one you are.

Can I have a log burner in a smoke control area?

Yes, as long as the stove is approved for smoke control areas and you burn dry wood. Gareth will check whether you’re in one on the survey and fit a stove that’s allowed.

How long does it take?

A full fireplace knockout takes two to three days. Gareth tells you how long your job will take on the survey.

Is the stove included?

On a full fireplace knockout, a twin-wall flue job and an extension, yes. On the two straight stove fits (£975 with the chimney stack in place, £1,375 with it removed) the stove is an extra cost. Gareth helps you choose one to suit the room and prices it with you on the survey.

Are the prices on the site fixed?

They’re Gareth’s guide prices for a typical job. After the free survey you get one confirmed price in writing before anything starts, so you know exactly where you stand.

What if you find a problem halfway through?

That’s what the survey is for: the chimney and the opening get looked at before you’re given a price. If something unexpected turns up once the breast is opened, Gareth shows you and tells you the cost before doing anything about it.

Do I need a deposit?

A deposit holds your fitting date and comes off the final bill. Gareth confirms the amount when he books you in. Nothing is paid on this website.

Is the paperwork sorted?

Yes. Every installation is signed off with building regs, and you keep the paperwork for your records.

What should I burn?

Dry wood: kiln-dried or well-seasoned logs. Wet wood tars the glass and the flue and gives off a lot less heat. If you’re not sure what you’ve got, ask Gareth when he lights your first fire.

Chimneys

How often does a chimney need sweeping?

Once a year for most people. If you burn wood hard all winter, twice is safer: once before the season and once part way through. Sweeping prices.

Do I get a certificate?

Yes, every sweep comes with one for your records. Home insurers and letting agents often ask for it.

My chimney isn’t used any more. Is that a problem?

It can be. An unused flue still lets water and birds in. Worth a look, and if it really isn’t needed, the stack can come down for good.
